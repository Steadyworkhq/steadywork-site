What the app does

The app does these things:

  • It interviews you about your work. The interview is a chat with an AI.
  • It produces a ranked list of tasks worth automating.
  • It runs automations. An automation reads your data and prepares drafts. A draft can be an email, a document, or a proposed entry in a system you have connected, such as a bill in your accounting system or a row in a spreadsheet.
  • If you set one up, it runs a chat on your website that answers your visitors. See Website chat.
  • It shows a dashboard: what your automations handled, numbers tracked from your data on each run, and an estimate of time saved. The estimate is based on your own figure. It is an estimate, not a measurement.

Approval and your responsibility

By default, nothing is sent, and nothing is changed in your systems, unless you approve that specific draft.

You can set an individual automation to act without waiting for your approval. If you do, it sends or enters what it prepares on its own, within the limits you set, and emails you a list after each run. You are responsible for everything it sends or enters this way, exactly as if you had approved each item. You can switch this off at any time.

When you approve an email, you choose what happens to it. It can be sent from your own mailbox. It can be placed in your Drafts folder. Or you can send it yourself. Each action is logged.

AI output can be wrong. It can misread your data or say something you would not say. Each draft is labelled either "Written by AI" or as made by fixed steps or a fixed program with no AI. Read each draft before you approve it. You are responsible for what you approve, including the messages that go out and the entries that are made.

Entries in your systems

An entry is something the app creates in a system you connected, such as a supplier bill in QuickBooks or a row in a sheet.

Every entry has its own confirmation screen. The screen lists exactly what will be created. Nothing is entered until you confirm. The one exception is an automation you have set to act without approval. It makes its entries on its own, within the limits you set, and emails you a list after each run.

Check the list before you confirm. You are responsible for the entries that are made.

Fixed programs

Once an automation has settled, the AI can write it down once as a small program. The program then runs without AI.

The app shows you a description of the program in plain words, a trial on your data, and a comparison with what the AI would have produced. You are responsible for what a program prepares, sends or enters, in the same way as for any other automation.

Fix it

If an automation breaks, you can ask the AI to find the problem and repair it. This uses your credits. A repair is tried on your data first. It is applied only if you choose to apply it.

If repeated repairs fail, Steadywork is alerted. A person at Steadywork may then look at that automation and its recent errors to help. We do not promise that every automation can be repaired.

Website chat

You can put a Steadywork chat on your own website. It answers your visitors automatically, using only the facts you wrote for it. You do not check each answer before it goes. Visitors are told the chat is automated.

Visitors' messages, and any name, email address or phone number they give, are stored so you can read them and follow up. When the chat cannot answer, it asks the visitor for contact details and creates a follow-up draft for you. Visitors' messages are sent to Anthropic to produce the answer.

If you use the chat:

  • You are responsible for the facts you give it. Keep them correct and current.
  • Each answer it gives uses your credits. You set a limit on new conversations a day. If your credits run out, it stops answering and asks visitors for their details instead.
  • You are responsible for telling your visitors how their data is used, in your own privacy notice.
  • You are responsible for having the chat on your website. AI can be wrong, even when it works only from your facts.

For your visitors' information, Steadywork acts for you and on your instructions. The Privacy Policy and the Data Processing Addendum say more.

The team owner can see all of the team's automations, and a dashboard for them. The dashboard does not track what people do outside the automations.

Transfer. The team owner can transfer an automation permanently to another member. Its rules, drafts and history go with it.

Whoever takes on an automation is shown a handover page, built from the automation's record. Whoever approves a draft is responsible for it. The team owner is responsible for the cover and transfer choices they make, including whose mailbox is used.
