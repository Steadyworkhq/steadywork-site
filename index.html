How it works

You don't need to know what can be automated. That's what the interview is for.

1

It interviews you

A short chat about your week: what you do, what repeats, what you dread. Your team can each do their own.

2

You get a ranked list

Every repetitive task you described, grouped and ordered by the hours it takes and how well it suits automation.

3

You switch one on

Say what it should prepare, in plain words. Point it at a report you already export, or connect a system.

4

You approve the drafts

On schedule, it prepares the emails or the report and waits. Review them together, edit any, approve, and they go.

The kind of work it takes on

Anything where you read a report, then write the same sort of message or summary every time. A few examples:

Chasing overdue invoices

Reads your unpaid invoices and drafts a polite reminder to each customer who's past due, in your wording.

The Monday numbers

Totals what each customer owes, compares it with last week, and writes the one-page brief you'd otherwise build by hand.

Quotes that went quiet

Finds quotes with no reply after a set number of days and drafts the follow-up for each.

What needs reordering

Reads the stock report and lists what's below its reorder point, grouped by supplier, ready to send.

Where the jobs stand

Turns the open-jobs sheet into a status update for the crew, the customer or the owner.

Whatever you describe

If it starts from data you already have and ends in an email or a document, it's a candidate. The interview finds them.

It works from reports you upload or email in. Direct connections to QuickBooks, Microsoft 365 (Outlook) and Smartsheet are being added during early access.

Built so you can trust it

Most people's worry about automation is the right one: what if it sends something wrong? So it can't.

Nothing happens without your approval

It prepares; you decide. See the full list of what would go out and to whom, change any of it, then approve. You choose whether approved emails are sent for you, placed in your Drafts folder, or left for you to send.

It tells you when AI is involved

Every draft is labelled.
Written by AI means read it carefully. Fixed program, no AI means the same data always gives the same result.

It learns from your corrections

Keep removing the same customer, or fixing the same line? It notices the pattern and offers to make it a standing rule.

It gets cheaper as it settles

Once an automation has been right for a while, it can be written down as a fixed program. From then on it runs without AI and uses none of your allowance.

Straight answers

Can it send something without me?

No. It prepares drafts and waits. Emails go out only for drafts you've approved, once each, and every send is recorded. You can also choose to have approved emails placed in your own Drafts folder, or to send them yourself.

Do I have to buy or learn new software?

No. It starts from reports you already export from the tools you run, sent in by upload or by email. You describe what you want in plain words.

Is my data safe?

Each account's data is kept apart from every other account's, and access to any system you connect is stored encrypted. Connections are read-only unless you choose to allow sending email. The AI work runs on Anthropic's Claude through its business service, which does not train on your data. Programs the AI writes for your account run in a sealed space with no access to the internet, your passwords or anyone else's data.

What does the AI get wrong?

It can misread a messy report or word something in a way you wouldn't. That's why every AI-written draft is labelled and waits for you, and why you can try any automation on your real data before saving it. When you correct the same thing twice, it offers to make that a rule.

What if I run out of credits?

Automations that the AI writes each time pause, and you get an email. Automations that run as fixed programs carry on, because they use none. You're never billed for extra use you didn't choose.

Can I cancel?

Any time. It's month to month, and you can take a copy of each automation's instructions, rules and program with you.
