Can QuickBooks automatically send invoice reminders?

Yes. Here are the exact steps, how to send one by hand, and the limits that catch people out.

Checked 2026-11-21. Menus in other products change, so a label may differ slightly on your screen.

Short answer: yes. QuickBooks Online can send invoice reminders on its own. Go to Settings, then Account and settings, then Sales. In the Reminders section, choose the edit icon, turn on automatic reminders and set when each one goes out. You can set up to three reminders, each a number of days before or after the due date.

How to turn on automatic invoice reminders

  1. Select the Settings gear, then Account and settings.
  2. Open the Sales tab. In some regions it is called Sales and Get Paid.
  3. In the Reminders section, select the edit (pencil) icon.
  4. Turn on automatic reminders, then turn on Reminder 1.
  5. Set its timing: a number of days, then Before or After the due date.
  6. Turn on Reminder 2 and Reminder 3 if you want them.
  7. Edit the subject line, greeting and message for each reminder, then save.

Intuit says you can schedule reminders up to 90 days before or after the invoice due date. A reminder goes out when an unpaid invoice matches your rule. Menu names change now and then, so if your screen looks different, search the help for "send email payment reminders for your invoices".

A sensible first setup is one reminder a few days before the due date, one a week after, and one two weeks after. Our guide on how often to send invoice reminders explains how to choose.

How to send a reminder by hand

You can also remind one customer at a time, which is useful when automatic reminders are off.

  1. Go to Sales (some accounts show All apps, then Sales and Get Paid), then Invoices.
  2. Find the unpaid invoice.
  3. In the Action column, choose Send reminder.
  4. Change the message if you want, then send it.

You choose who gets it and you can read it first. The cost is that you have to remember to do it. A weekly look at your overdue invoices is enough.

What the built-in reminders cannot do

  • One set of wording and timing for everyone. A customer who is three days late for the first time reads the same message as one who is late every month.
  • Only unpaid invoices that were emailed from QuickBooks. Intuit says automatic reminders go out only for invoices you have already emailed. If you downloaded the PDF and sent it yourself, nothing is reminded.
  • No way to leave one customer out on the standard plans. Intuit staff say in the community forum that the setting applies to the whole company. The customer on a payment plan or in a dispute gets the same reminder as everyone else.
  • Three reminders at most, within 90 days either side of the due date.
  • You do not see each one before it goes.

Leaving a customer out on QuickBooks Online Advanced

QuickBooks Online Advanced is the top plan. Intuit's help "Use workflows in QuickBooks Online Advanced to send reminders" says you can set conditions for when a reminder goes out and to whom, including a customer condition. The customer is either "within All" or specific customers you select. So a reminder workflow can send to a chosen list of customers.

The list names who is included, not who is left out. A new customer gets no reminders until you add them. Check the list now and then. If you use a workflow, check whether the standard setting is still on, so no one gets two reminders. The full steps are in our guide on stopping QuickBooks invoice reminders for one customer.

Check that it is working

After you save, give it a few days. If nothing goes out, check three things: the invoice was emailed from QuickBooks, it is still unpaid, and the customer has an email address on file. Also check the dates: a reminder set for 7 days after the due date will not go out for an invoice that is only 3 days late.

A reminder on an invoice the customer says they paid usually means the payment was received but never recorded against that invoice. Record the payment and check that the invoice shows Paid. A short, friendly apology settles it.

When the built-in reminders are enough

If you have a small number of customers on the same terms, and you are happy for each of them to get the same note, turn the built-in reminders on and stop there. They work, and they run without you.

If you keep thinking "but not that customer", or you want to read each reminder before it goes, you need something that prepares reminders for approval. If a reminder needs a stronger tone, see how to chase overdue invoices by email.

A note on late fees

Late fees and their amounts depend on your agreement with the customer and on state law. Only mention a consequence you intend to carry out. This is not legal advice.
