Payment reminder email for electricians

Electrical invoices stall for their own reasons: inspections, progress billing, builders and retainage. Here is a schedule and wording that fit.

Checked 2026-11-21. Menus in other products change, so a label may differ slightly on your screen.

Short answer: send a reminder a few days before the invoice is due, another on the due date, then follow up at about 7, 14 and 30 days late. On electrical work, add one step most trades do not need: ask where the inspection or permit sign-off stands, because many customers hold payment until it passes. Name the invoice, the amount and a way to pay in every message.

Why electrical invoices stall

Often the delay has a reason built into the job:

  • The inspection. A panel upgrade or a rough-in is not finished until the inspector signs off. Homeowners and builders often say "we'll pay once it passes." Some contracts do tie final payment to a passed inspection or a certificate of occupancy. Check yours.
  • Progress billing. On bigger jobs you bill in stages against a schedule of values, which is your price split into line items such as rough-in, trim-out and gear. Each stage has its own invoice, so there is more to lose track of.
  • Builders and general contractors. They often pay you only after they have been paid by the owner. Your due date and their payment run may not line up.
  • Retainage. Part of your payment may be held back until the job ends. More on that below.
  • Small service calls. A $180 breaker swap does not feel worth a chase, so it never gets one. Ten of those add up.

Retainage, lien and late-fee rules vary by state and contract, so read your own agreement. This is not legal advice.

A reminder schedule that fits electrical work

Service calls and small repairs

Invoice the same day. Send the reminder on the due date, and a nudge about 7 days later. These customers usually just forgot. Keep the message to three lines. Invoice before you leave the site, while the customer is standing there, and send it by email or text with a pay link that opens and works on a phone. A small invoice that goes out three days later is the one that gets forgotten.

Jobs that need an inspection

Put the inspection date on the invoice or in the email. If payment is waiting on it, do not reword the same reminder. Ask: "Has the inspector been out? If it passed, we can close this out." Book the inspection yourself where you can, since a delayed inspection is a delayed payment.

Progress billing

Send each progress invoice as soon as the stage is done, and attach the schedule of values showing percent complete. Remind a few days before the due date, since a customer who pays on a set run needs your invoice approved before it. Follow up at 7, 14 and 30 days.

Builders and general contractors

Write to the person who approves and pays bills, not only the project manager you work with. Ask whether the invoice was approved and when the next payment run is. If their contract with you says they pay after they are paid, ask for the date they expect payment, and write it down.

Reminding for retainage

Retainage is the share of your payment that the customer holds back until the job is finished, as a safeguard that the work is complete. It is not late money. It is owed on a date or an event, so remind on that date or event, not on a normal overdue schedule.

  • Record the amount held and what releases it, such as the final inspection or the punch list sign-off.
  • When that happens, send the release invoice right away, and refer to the contract clause.
  • Put a calendar note on the release date and follow up like any other invoice from there.

Use the percentages and release dates in your own agreement.

Email templates to copy

1. Before or on the due date

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] for [job address] is due [due date] Hi [name], A reminder that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] is due on [due date]. A copy is attached. You can pay here: [pay link] If you need anything from us to approve it, such as a permit copy or inspection record, just say so and I'll send it today. Thank you, [your name]

2. Overdue, first follow-up

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] was due [due date] Hi [name], Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date] and I don't see a payment yet. A copy is attached. You can pay here: [pay link] If it's already on its way, thank you, and please ignore this. If something is holding it up, tell me and I'll sort it out. [your name] [phone]

3. Firmer, waiting on an inspection or approval

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] is [days] days overdue Hi [name], Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date]. I understand it may be waiting on the inspection or on approval on your side. Could you tell me where it stands? If the inspection has passed, we can close this out. If it hasn't been scheduled, I can help book it. Please let me know the date you expect to pay. You can pay here: [pay link] [your name] [phone]

4. Final notice

Subject: Final notice: invoice [invoice number] Hi [name], Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] is now [days] days overdue, and I haven't had a reply to my earlier messages. Please pay by [date]. If I don't hear from you by then, I will [pause further work / apply the late fee in our agreement / pass this on for collection]. If something has gone wrong, call me on [phone] and we can agree a plan. [your name]

Only state a consequence you are entitled to and willing to carry out. Late fees and their amounts depend on your agreement with the customer and on state law.

A short text for small service calls

Text only customers who agreed to receive texts from you, for example when they booked the call. Keep it short and give them an easy way to say stop.

Hi [name], it's [your name] at [business]. A reminder that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] is due [due date]. Pay here: [pay link]. Reply STOP to opt out.

What the tools you already have can do

QuickBooks Online can send reminders on its own. Go to Settings, then Account and settings, then the Sales tab, and turn on Automatic invoice reminders in the Reminders section. Every customer gets the same wording, though, and it knows nothing about inspections or retainage. Standard QuickBooks Online reminders also cannot leave one customer out. QuickBooks Online Advanced can, using workflows. That helps if you handle one builder by phone and do not want them emailed. Our guide on QuickBooks invoice reminders has the steps, and stopping reminders for one customer covers the options.
