How to ask for payment politely

Asking for money feels awkward, so it gets put off. Being clear and friendly is kinder to both sides, and it makes it easy for them to pay.

Checked 2026-11-21. Menus in other products change, so a label may differ slightly on your screen.

Short answer: be direct and kind at the same time. Say what the payment is for, the amount, the due date, how to pay and who to contact, in plain words, with the invoice attached. Skip the apologies and the hints. A clear request is easier for the client to act on, and it spares you both a second awkward email.

Why people put it off

Many people who run a small business dislike asking for money. It feels pushy, or like doubting the client. So they wait a few more days, then send something vague like "just checking in", and the invoice sits.

Being direct is kinder to both sides. The client may simply have forgotten, lost the email or not known the date. A plain request with the amount and a way to pay lets them fix it in two minutes. A vague one makes them guess what you want and leaves you wondering whether to write again. Asking is also not rude: you did the work, and paying for it is part of the deal.

The five things every request needs

  1. What it is for. The job, the invoice number or the date of the work, so they can match it to their records.
  2. The amount. The exact figure, in the message itself, not only in the attachment.
  3. The due date. The original date, and a new one if you are asking for it sooner.
  4. How to pay. A pay link, or your bank details, or where to send a check. One clear way is better than five.
  5. Who to contact. A named person with a phone number, so a problem gets raised with you rather than ignored.

Attach the invoice every time, even on the third message. Nobody wants to search their inbox for it.

Tone: warm in manner, plain in content

Politeness lives in how you say it, not in how much you leave out. Greet them by name, assume the best, thank them, and then state the facts. A good test is to read the message as the client. Could they pay in the next two minutes without writing back to ask anything? If not, something from the list above is missing.

Keep each message short. One request per email is easier to answer than three. And send it from your own address, with your name at the bottom, so it reads as coming from a person.

Phrases that work

  • "Invoice 1042 for $850 is due on March 3."
  • "You can pay here: [link]."
  • "If anything is wrong with the invoice, tell me and I'll fix it."
  • "Could you let me know by Friday when to expect payment?"
  • "If it's already on its way, thank you."

Phrases to avoid

  • "Just checking in" with no amount. It does not say what you want, so it is easy to ignore.
  • Over-apologizing. "Sorry to bother you, I hate to ask" tells the client that paying is optional. One polite line is enough.
  • Vague threats. "Further action may be taken" sounds hostile and means nothing. If you name a consequence, make it one your terms allow and that you will carry out.
  • Guilt or sarcasm. "As I mentioned before" and "per my last email" make people defensive.

Late fees and the amounts you can charge depend on your agreement with the customer and on state law. This guide is not legal advice.

Five templates for different moments

1. Before the due date

A heads-up a few days before. It is not a chase, and many clients like it.

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] for [amount], due [due date] Hi [name], A friendly heads-up that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] for [what the work was] is due on [due date]. A copy is attached. You can pay here: [pay link] If you need anything from me to get it paid, just reply to this email. Thank you, [your name]

2. Just overdue

Send this a few days after the due date. Assume it slipped their mind.

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] was due on [due date] Hi [name], Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date], and I don't see it as paid yet. A copy is attached. You can pay here: [pay link] If it's already on its way, thank you, and please ignore this. If something is wrong with the invoice, tell me and I'll fix it. [your name] [phone]

3. A good, long-standing customer

Keep it warm and short, but still give the amount and date.

Subject: Invoice [invoice number], a quick note Hi [name], Thank you for being such a good customer. I noticed that invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was due on [due date] and is still open. I'm guessing it slipped through, which happens to all of us. A copy is attached, and you can pay here: [pay link] Thanks, as always. [your name]

4. A client who has gone quiet

Ask for a reply, not just a payment. Only offer a payment plan if you are willing to agree to one.

Subject: Invoice [invoice number]: can you tell me where it stands? Hi [name], I've written about invoice [invoice number] for [amount], due [due date], and haven't heard back. I'd like to get this settled. Could you reply by [reply date] to tell me when I can expect payment, or what is holding it up? If it's easier, call me on [phone]. If you'd like to split it into two payments, I'm happy to talk about that. You can pay here: [pay link] [your name]

5. A deposit before work starts

Put the deposit in writing before you order materials or hold the date. What is reasonable depends on the job, and a few states limit deposits on some kinds of work, so check your own rules.

Subject: Deposit to book your [job] on [start date] Hi [name], Thank you for choosing us for [job]. To book your slot on [start date], we ask for a deposit of [amount], which is [percent] of the total of [total]. The rest is due [balance terms]. You can pay the deposit here: [pay link] Once it's received, I'll confirm the date and order any materials. If you have a question about the deposit, reply here or call me on [phone]. [your name]

Asking in person or by phone

A call is often the quickest way to settle an invoice that email has not moved. Have the invoice number and amount in front of you. Then say something like:

  • "Hi [name], it's [your name] from [business]. I'm calling about invoice [number] for [amount], which was due on [date]."
  • "Can you tell me when I can expect it?"
  • "I'll send the invoice and pay link again right now, so you have it."

If you reach voicemail, leave the same three facts: who you are, which invoice and amount, and your number. Stay calm and friendly even if they are short with you. If they say there is a problem, ask what it is and write it down, then follow up in writing.

Write down the date they give you and send a short email the same day confirming it. If you plan to text instead, only text customers who have agreed to be texted.
