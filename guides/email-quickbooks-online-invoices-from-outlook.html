How to email QuickBooks Online invoices from Outlook

QuickBooks Online will email your invoices for you, but not from your Outlook mailbox. Here is what it can do, the manual way round it, and a way to send a whole batch after you have checked them.

Checked 2026-11-18. Menus in other products change, so a label may differ slightly on your screen.

Short answer: QuickBooks Online sends invoices from its own mail address, or from one Gmail address if you connect it. Intuit's help pages say other providers, including Outlook, are not supported yet. To send from Outlook you save each invoice as a PDF and attach it yourself, or use a tool that does that step for you.

What QuickBooks Online can do on its own

  • Send one invoice: open the invoice and choose Save and send. The customer gets an email from QuickBooks with your business name on it. Replies come to the address in your company settings.
  • Send several at once: go to Sales, then Invoices. Tick the invoices you want, open Batch actions and choose Send.
  • Send from Gmail: an admin can connect one Gmail address for the whole company. Intuit notes some limits, such as attachments, and users report that batch sends can still go out from the QuickBooks address.

What it cannot do is send from an Outlook or Microsoft 365 mailbox. That matters if your customers know your address, if QuickBooks emails land in their junk folder, or if you want the sent invoice in your own Sent Items.

The manual way to send from Outlook

  1. In QuickBooks Online, open the invoice and choose Print or Preview, then download it as a PDF.
  2. In Outlook, start a new email to the customer and attach the PDF.
  3. Write the subject and message. Include the invoice number, the amount and the due date.
  4. Send it, then go back to QuickBooks and mark the invoice as sent so your records match.
  5. Repeat for every invoice.

This works well for three invoices. For thirty it is an hour of careful, boring work, and it is easy to attach the wrong PDF to the wrong customer.

Why the usual automation tools fall short here

Tools like Zapier can send an email when an invoice is created. The trouble is that many offices review invoices by hand before they go out. An automation that fires the moment an invoice is created sends it before anyone has looked. What you want is the opposite: review first, then send everything that passed with one click.
