QuickBooks recurring invoice not sending: what to check

A recurring invoice that does not go out is usually one setting. Here is a checklist to find it, and how to pause or stop one.

Checked 2026-11-21. Menus in other products change, so a label may differ slightly on your screen.

Short answer: a recurring invoice in QuickBooks Online usually fails to go out for one of a few reasons. The template type is not Scheduled, the setting that sends emails automatically is off, the template has passed its end date, or the customer's email is missing or wrong. Open Recurring transactions, check those four things first, then look in the customer's junk folder.

Where to look first

All the settings below live on the recurring template. Select the Settings gear, then Recurring transactions, in the Lists column. Some accounts reach it through All apps, then Accounting, then Recurring transactions. Find the customer's template, open the Action column and choose Edit. Screens differ a little between plans and change over time, so use the names here as a guide.

The checklist

1. The template type is not Scheduled

This is the most common cause. Intuit describes three types. Scheduled creates and sends the transaction on its own. Reminder creates it at set times, but you edit and send it yourself. Unscheduled is only a saved template that you use by hand. Only Scheduled templates run by themselves. Check the Type box, set it to Scheduled, and select Save template.

2. The setting that sends emails automatically is off

A Scheduled template can still create an invoice and not email it. In the template settings, turn on Automatically send emails (some versions call it Send later). Save, then reopen the template to confirm it stuck, since some users report it switching back to off after edits.

3. The customer has no email, or the wrong one

Open the customer record (Sales, then Customers) and look at the Email field. A blank or mistyped address means nothing reaches them. Fix it, then check the template picked up the new address. Intuit's pages do not say exactly what QuickBooks does when the address is blank, so check it rather than assume.

4. The start date has not arrived, or the end date has passed

Templates only run automatically if the end setting has not been reached. Check the start date and the next date shown for the template. Under End, choose None, or a date or number of occurrences that has not passed. QuickBooks runs most transactions early in the morning Pacific time, but Intuit notes they can run later than expected, so give it until the end of the day before you worry.

5. The template is paused

If someone paused the template, it will not run. In the Recurring transactions list, open the Action dropdown. If it shows an option to resume, use it. Another way people stop a template is by switching it to Unscheduled, which takes you back to item 1. Intuit's pages do not document the pause option well, so confirm what you see on your screen.

6. The customer is inactive

If the customer was made inactive, open the customer list, choose to include inactive customers and make them active again. Then check the template.

7. The email went out but landed in junk

Intuit says this is a common reason customers do not get QuickBooks emails. Ask the customer to check their junk or spam folder and add your sending address to their safe senders list. Intuit also suggests sending yourself a test and retyping your own company email under Settings, then Account and settings, then Company, then Contact info. Customers whose mail is run by an IT company can ask them to allowlist QuickBooks mail servers; Intuit publishes the list.

8. Something is wrong with the template itself

If the next date moves forward but no invoice appears, open the template and select Save template. Saving can show the error. If nothing helps, Intuit's advice is to create a new Scheduled template with the same details, then delete the old one so you do not get duplicates.

Send the missed invoice by hand

Do not wait for the next cycle. In Recurring transactions, open the Action dropdown on the template and choose Use. Check the details, then save and send the invoice yourself. If you want a copy of the wording, here is a short note to the customer.

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] for [amount] Hi [name], Your invoice [invoice number] for [amount] was meant to reach you on [date] and may not have arrived. I have attached it here. You can pay online: [pay link] It is due on [due date]. Please let me know if you have any trouble. Thanks, [your name]

How to pause or stop a recurring invoice

There are three ways, from lightest to final.

  1. Pause it. In the Recurring transactions list, open the Action dropdown for the template and choose Pause. This is how Intuit's staff describe it in the community forum. You have to do it template by template.
  2. Change the type to Unscheduled. Select Edit, change Type to Unscheduled (Reminder also works), and select Save template. The template stays on file and you can switch it back to Scheduled later.
  3. Delete it. In the Action column, open the dropdown, choose Delete and confirm. Invoices already created stay as they are. Only future ones stop.

If a customer has left, deleting the template is cleaner. It also lets you make the customer inactive afterward.

What the built-in tools cannot do

You may not notice a recurring invoice failed to send until a customer asks where it is. If you want to read each invoice before it goes out, use the Reminder type. The trade-off is that you send each one by hand. Either way, a short monthly look at the invoice list and Recurring transactions catches problems early.
